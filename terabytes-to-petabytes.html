Terabytes vs. Petabytes: The 1024× Factor

Storage vendors quote capacity in decimal terabytes (10¹² bytes), while operating systems report in binary tebibytes (2⁴⁰ bytes). The 1,024 multiplier between TB and PB is the binary standard used by filesystems, volume managers, and RAID controllers — 1 PB = 1,024 TB = 1,125,899,906,842,624 bytes.

When provisioning a 10 PB Ceph cluster, you need at least 10,240 TB of raw capacity before erasure-coding overhead. With 8+3 EC, the usable capacity drops to ~72% of raw — meaning a 10 PB target actually requires ~14.2 PB raw, or ~14,540 TB of physical disk.
